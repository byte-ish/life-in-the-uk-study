# Chapter 2: What is the UK? cheat sheet

<p class="lead">Which places are in the UK, which are in Great Britain, and which are linked to the UK but not part of it, plus the official name and who governs.</p>

## How to use this

This sheet follows Chapter 2 of the official handbook, *Life in the United Kingdom: A guide for new residents* (3rd edition), still the only testable source. The handbook's Chapter 2 is a single page, so every sentence of it is covered here.

- Chapter 2 has no dates, so the tables sort places and names. Use **Hide answers** to test yourself on each yes/no.
- **Bold** = a key word. Gold rows marked **★ Often asked** = what comes up most in practice tests. This is based on unofficial study guides, as the Home Office does not publish question data.
- Rows marked **Today (not in the handbook)** show changes since 2013. Newer printings of the handbook may already include some of these changes, so if your copy differs from this sheet, follow your copy.

**GB = 3, UK = 4.** Great Britain is England, Scotland and Wales. Add Northern Ireland and you have the UK, which is why the official name ends "and Northern Ireland".
{: .hook}

## 1. In or out? The places to know
{: .era-3}

The UK is made up of four countries. These places are the ones the handbook names.

| Place | In the UK? | In Great Britain? | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| England | **Yes** | **Yes** | One of the four countries |
| Scotland | **Yes** | **Yes** | One of the four countries |
| Wales | **Yes** | **Yes** | One of the four countries |
| Northern Ireland | **Yes** | **No**{: .hy} | The only country in the UK that is **not** in Great Britain |
| The rest of Ireland | No | No | An **independent country** |
| Channel Islands | No | No | Closely linked, **not part**{: .hy} of the UK: a Crown dependency |
| Isle of Man | No | No | Closely linked, **not part**{: .hy} of the UK: a Crown dependency |
| St Helena | No | No | A British overseas territory |
| Falkland Islands | No | No | A British overseas territory |

## 2. The names
{: .era-4}

The handbook defines three names, and explains how it uses "Britain" and "British".

| Term | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland | **The official name**{: .hy} of the country | Great Britain **and** Northern Ireland |
| Great Britain | **England, Scotland and Wales** only, **not**{: .hy} Northern Ireland | GB is three countries, the UK is four |
| The UK | **England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland**{: .hy} | Four countries |
| "Britain", "British Isles", "British" | Used **in the handbook** to refer to **everyone in the UK** | In this book they all mean the whole UK |

Outside the handbook, "British Isles" usually means the whole group of islands, including Ireland. That isn't tested: for the test, go by the handbook.
{: .note}

The 1801 name, the **United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland** (Chapter 3), is not today's name. Ireland split in 1922, so the name now ends "and **Northern** Ireland".
{: .hook}

## 3. Linked to the UK, but not part of it
{: .era-6}

Some places are closely linked to the UK but are not part of it.

| Group | Examples | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crown dependencies | **Channel Islands**, **Isle of Man** | Closely linked with the UK but **not part of it**; they have **their own governments**{: .hy} | Close to home, own governments |
| British overseas territories | **St Helena**{: .hy}, **Falkland Islands** | In **other parts of the world**; linked to the UK but **not part of it** | Overseas = far away; a "choose two" favourite |
| The rest of Ireland | (none) | An **independent country** | Not a Crown dependency or a territory |

**C**hannel Islands and Isle of Man = **C**rown dependencies, **c**lose to home with their own governments. **O**verseas territories are in **o**ther parts of the world.
{: .hook}

Some questions ask you to choose **two** answers. The official practice questions do this for overseas territories: the two the handbook names are **St Helena** and the **Falkland Islands**.
{: .note}

## 4. Who governs
{: .era-7}

One parliament governs the whole UK, and three parts of it also have their own.

| Who | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The UK | Governed by **the parliament sitting in Westminster**{: .hy} | Westminster = the whole UK |
| Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland | Also have **parliaments or assemblies** of their own, with **devolved powers in defined areas** | Devolved = some powers handed down, only in defined areas |
| Their names (Chapter 5) | **Scottish Parliament**, **National Assembly for Wales** (also called the **Welsh Assembly**), **Northern Ireland Assembly** | Scotland has a parliament; Wales and Northern Ireland have assemblies |
| Newer printings | The current printing calls the Welsh members **Members of the Senedd (MSs)**; the body has been called the **Senedd** (Welsh Parliament) since 2020 | Older printings: National Assembly for Wales, AMs |

## 6. Commonly confused
{: .era-none}

Most wrong answers in this chapter come from mixing up the UK and Great Britain, or the two kinds of linked places.

| Pair | Tell them apart |
| --- | --- |
| **The UK** vs **Great Britain** | UK = England, Scotland, Wales **and Northern Ireland**; Great Britain = England, Scotland and Wales only |
| **Northern Ireland** vs **the rest of Ireland** | Northern Ireland is in the UK; the rest of Ireland is an **independent country** |
| **Crown dependencies** vs **British overseas territories** | Crown dependencies: Channel Islands and Isle of Man, with their own governments. Overseas territories: e.g. St Helena and the Falkland Islands, in other parts of the world. **Neither** is part of the UK |
| **Today's official name** vs **the 1801 name** | United Kingdom of Great Britain and **Northern Ireland** (today) vs United Kingdom of Great Britain and **Ireland** (1801, Chapter 3) |
| **Westminster** vs **devolved** bodies | Westminster governs the whole UK; Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have parliaments or assemblies with powers in defined areas |
| **"British Isles"** in the handbook vs in geography | The handbook uses it for everyone in the UK; geography usually includes Ireland too |

If a question asks whether a place is "part of the UK", only England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are. The Channel Islands, the Isle of Man, St Helena and the Falkland Islands are only *linked*.
{: .hook}
