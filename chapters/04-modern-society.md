## How to use this

This sheet follows Chapter 4 of the official handbook, *Life in the United Kingdom: A guide for new residents* (3rd edition), still the only testable source. Every fact here was checked against the handbook text.

- Chapter 4 is a big part of the test: **119 of the 390** official 2013 practice questions come from it.
- **Bold** = a key name, date or number. Gold rows marked **★ Often asked** = topics the official practice questions ask about, most of them more than once.
- Most tables here match a name to a fact. Use **Hide answers** to test yourself: the name stays visible and the fact is blurred.
- Updated to the **current printing** (3rd edition, 28th impression, 2026), using the text of the 2019 printing and TSO's [list of amendments](https://tsoshop.co.uk/gempdf/Life_in_the_UK_latest_impression_amendments%20050126.pdf) since then. Where a figure changed, the row says what older printings said.
- The current printing also updates the ski centres, the National Eisteddfod's location, the Turner Prize venues, TV licences and the number of National Trust volunteers. Those rows give today's position from official sources, marked "checked online"; the printing's own wording may differ slightly.
- The handbook says you **will not need to remember dates of birth or death**, so the years in brackets after people's names are for context only.
- Rows marked **Today (not in the handbook)** show changes since 2013. Newer printings of the handbook may already include some of these changes, so if your copy differs from this sheet, follow your copy.

{: .era-1}

A more diverse society than 100 years ago, in both ethnic and religious terms.

## 2. Religion and patron saints
{: .era-2}

The UK is historically a Christian country, and everyone has the legal right to choose their religion, or to choose not to practise one.

| No religion | **25%**{: .hy} | Second biggest answer (older printings: 21%) |
| Muslim | **4.8%** | |
| Hindu | **1.5%** | |
| Sikh | **0.8%** | |
| Jewish or Buddhist | **Less than 0.5%** each | |

| Its head | The **monarch** | The monarch can select the Archbishop and senior officials, but usually the **Prime Minister** and a Church committee choose |

{: .era-3}

The festival calendar, from New Year to Hogmanay.

## 8. Commonly confused
{: .era-none}

This chapter is full of look-alike names. These are the pairs worth drilling.

| Pair | Tell them apart |
| --- | --- |
| **St David** vs **St Patrick** (both March) | David = **1 March**, Wales; Patrick = **17 March**, Northern Ireland |
| **St George** vs **St Andrew** | George = **23 April**, England; Andrew = **30 November**, Scotland |
| **Thistle** vs **shamrock** | Thistle = Scotland; shamrock = Northern Ireland |
| **Gaelic** vs **Irish Gaelic** | Gaelic = parts of Scotland's Highlands and Islands; Irish Gaelic = Northern Ireland |
| **Church of England** vs **Church of Scotland** | England: the state Church, head = monarch, spiritual leader = Archbishop of Canterbury. Scotland: national, **Presbyterian**, chaired by a **Moderator** for one year |
| **Hogmanay** vs **New Year's Day** | Hogmanay = **31 December** in Scotland; New Year's Day = 1 January (Scotland also has 2 January off) |
| **Mothering Sunday** vs **Father's Day** | Mothering Sunday = 3 weeks before **Easter**; Father's Day = **third Sunday in June** |
| **Shrove Tuesday** vs **Ash Wednesday** | Shrove Tuesday = pancakes, the day before Lent; Ash Wednesday = Lent begins |
| **Diwali** vs **Hannukah** vs **Vaisakhi** | Diwali: Hindus and Sikhs, 5 days, lights. Hannukah: Jews, 8 days, menorah. Vaisakhi: Sikhs, 14 April, the Khalsa |
| **Eid al-Fitr** vs **Eid ul Adha** | al-Fitr = end of **Ramadan**; ul Adha = the prophet **Ibrahim** |
| **Bonfire Night** vs **Remembrance Day** | 5 November, Guy Fawkes, 1605 vs 11 November, poppies, the First World War |
| **Grand National** vs **Scottish Grand National** vs **Royal Ascot** | Aintree (near Liverpool) vs Ayr vs Berkshire (5 days) |
| **Chichester** vs **Knox-Johnston** vs **MacArthur** | First alone (1966/67) vs first non-stop vs fastest (2004) |
| **Tanni Grey-Thompson** vs **David Weir** | Both wheelchair athletes who won the London Marathon: Tanni six times, Weir eight. Tanni: 16 Paralympic medals over five Games; Weir: six golds over two Games |
| **Chris Hoy** vs **Bradley Wiggins** | Both cyclists. Hoy: Scottish, six Olympic golds. Wiggins: first Briton to win the **Tour de France** |
| **Turner** vs **Constable** | Both landscape painters. Turner: modern style, the Turner Prize. Constable: **Dedham Vale** |
| **Inigo Jones** vs **Wren** vs **Adam** vs **Lutyens** | Queen's House and Banqueting House vs St Paul's vs Dumfries House vs the Cenotaph and New Delhi |
| **Capability Brown** vs **Gertrude Jekyll** | Natural landscapes around country houses vs colourful gardens around Lutyens's houses |
| **Mercury** vs **Brit Awards** vs **Turner** vs **Olivier** vs **BAFTA** vs **Booker** | Album vs music categories vs contemporary art vs theatre vs film vs fiction |
| **Proms** vs **Edinburgh Fringe** vs **National Eisteddfod** | BBC classical music, Royal Albert Hall vs theatre and comedy in Edinburgh vs Welsh-language culture |
| **Lake District** vs **Snowdonia** vs **Loch Lomond** | England's largest park (Windermere) vs North Wales (Snowdon) vs west of Scotland (largest fresh water in mainland Britain) |
| **Kew** and **Sissinghurst** vs **Bodnant** vs **Mount Stewart** | England vs Wales vs Northern Ireland |
| **16** vs **18** | Lottery, and wine or beer with a meal = 16; buying alcohol and gambling = 18 |

If a question names a famous British person, ask first: **sport, music, art, building or book?** Then match within that table.
