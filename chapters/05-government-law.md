## How to use this

This sheet follows Chapter 5 of the official handbook, *Life in the United Kingdom: A guide for new residents* (3rd edition), still the only testable source. Every fact was checked against the handbook text.

- Chapter 5 is a big part of the test: **121 of the 390** official 2013 practice questions come from it.
- **Bold** = a key name, date or number. Gold rows marked **★ Often asked** = topics the official practice questions ask about, most of them more than once.
- **This is the chapter most changed since 2013.** TSO says the handbook's sections on the UK's EU membership and on Queen Elizabeth II and the monarchy "have been removed from subsequent prints" ([TSO](https://tsoshop.co.uk/article/636421/Life-in-the-United-Kingdom-A-Guide-for-New-Residents-3rd-Edition)). TSO's [list of amendments](https://tsoshop.co.uk/gempdf/Life_in_the_UK_latest_impression_amendments%20050126.pdf) shows that printings from the 23rd impression say **the King**, with a paragraph on Queen Elizabeth II's death. So this sheet leaves out the EU material, and shows the 2013 monarchy wording next to today's. Newer printings may word these parts differently: **if your copy of the handbook differs from this sheet, follow the handbook.**
- Updated to the **current printing** (3rd edition, 28th impression, 2026), using the text of the 2019 printing and TSO's [list of amendments](https://tsoshop.co.uk/gempdf/Life_in_the_UK_latest_impression_amendments%20050126.pdf) since then. Where a figure changed, the row says what older printings said.
- The current printing also updates how to contact elected members, the electoral register, polling stations, the Scottish Parliament and applying for a National Insurance number online, and removes the National Citizen Service. Those rows give today's position from official sources, marked "checked online"; the printing's own wording may differ slightly.
- Rows marked **Today (not in the handbook)** show changes since 2013, each checked online and linked. Newer printings of the handbook may already include some of these changes, so if your copy differs from this sheet, follow your copy.

## 1. Democracy and the constitution
{: .era-1}

Democracy is a system of government where the whole adult population gets a say, by voting directly or by choosing representatives.

| When | Event | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turn of the 19th century | Britain is not yet a democracy: only **men over 21 who owned a certain amount of property** could vote | Property needed |
| 19th century | The **franchise** (the number of people with the right to vote) grows; parties begin to involve ordinary men and women | Franchise = who can vote |
| **1830s and 1840s** | The **Chartists**{: .hy} campaign for six changes: a vote for **every man**, **elections every year**, all regions **equal** in the electoral system, **secret ballots**, **any man** able to stand as an MP, and **MPs to be paid** | Seen as a failure at the time |
| **1918** | **Most** of the Chartists' reforms have been adopted; women **over 30** get the vote | |
| **1928** | **Men and women over 21** can vote: a fully democratic voting system | Equal at 21 |
| **1969** | Voting age reduced to **18**{: .hy} for men and women | The present voting age |

| Topic | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A constitution | The principles by which a country is governed: its institutions, laws and conventions, and how their power is kept in check | |
| The British constitution | **Not written down in any single document**{: .hy}, so it is described as **"unwritten"** | The UK never had a revolution that permanently created a new system |
| The main parts of government | The **monarchy**; **Parliament** (House of Commons and House of Lords); the **Prime Minister**; the **cabinet**; the **judiciary** (courts); the **police**; the **civil service**; **local government** | Plus devolved governments in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland |

**Six Chartist demands**: every man a vote, annual elections, equal regions, secret ballots, any man an MP, paid MPs. "Every man voting secretly, every year, for paid MPs from equal regions."
{: .hook}

## 2. The monarchy
{: .era-2}

The UK is a parliamentary democracy with the monarch as head of state, and a **constitutional monarchy**.

| Topic | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constitutional monarchy | The monarch **does not rule** the country but **appoints the government** the people have chosen in a democratic election | Reigns, does not rule |
| Choosing the Prime Minister | The monarch invites the leader of the party with the **most MPs**, or of a **coalition**, to become Prime Minister | |
| Meetings with the PM | Regular meetings; the monarch can **advise, warn and encourage**{: .hy}, but the **PM and cabinet** decide policy | Advise, warn, encourage |
| Opening Parliament | The monarch **opens each new parliamentary session**{: .hy} with a speech summarising the government's policies for the year ahead | A ceremonial role |
| Acts of Parliament | All are made **in the monarch's name**{: .hy} | |
| Representing the UK | Receives **foreign ambassadors and high commissioners**, entertains visiting heads of state, makes **state visits** overseas | |
| Stability | Provides **stability and continuity** while governments change; a focus for **national identity and pride** | |
| National Anthem | Played at important national occasions and at events attended by the monarch or the Royal Family | |
| Citizenship ceremony | New citizens **swear** (oath) or **affirm** (affirmation) **loyalty to the monarch**, "faithful and bear true allegiance… according to law" | Oath = by Almighty God; affirmation = without |

| 2013 text (removed or updated in later prints) | Today (verified) | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Queen Elizabeth II**{: .hy} is head of state; reigned since her father's death in **1952**; **Diamond Jubilee** in **2012** (60 years as queen) | Queen Elizabeth II died on **8 September 2022**; **King Charles III** is the monarch ([TSO](https://tsoshop.co.uk/article/636421/Life-in-the-United-Kingdom-A-Guide-for-New-Residents-3rd-Edition)) | The official 2013 practice set asked about 1952 and the Diamond Jubilee |
| Married to **Prince Philip, the Duke of Edinburgh**; heir: **Prince Charles, the Prince of Wales** | Prince Charles became King Charles III | |
| National Anthem: **"God Save the Queen"** | **"God Save the King"**, now that the monarch is a king ([royal.uk](https://www.royal.uk/national-anthem)) | Only "Queen" became "King" and the pronouns changed |
| Allegiance to "Her Majesty Queen Elizabeth the Second, her Heirs and Successors" | Allegiance to "King Charles III, his Heirs and Successors" ([GOV.UK guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/645a174cc6e8970012a0fadf/Citizenship_Ceremonies_Guidance_Notes_2023.pdf)) | |

TSO's [list of amendments](https://tsoshop.co.uk/gempdf/Life_in_the_UK_latest_impression_amendments%20050126.pdf) confirms that printings from the 23rd impression change every reference to the Queen to the King and add a paragraph on Queen Elizabeth II's death; the 26th removes the word "Consort".
{: .note}

The monarch's three verbs with the Prime Minister: **advise, warn, encourage**. The decisions belong to the PM and cabinet.
{: .hook}

## 3. Parliament and elections
{: .era-3}

The UK is divided into **constituencies**. Voters in each one elect an MP at a General Election, and all the MPs form the House of Commons.

| Topic | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parliament | **The House of Commons and the House of Lords**{: .hy} | Two chambers |
| The House of Commons | The **more important** chamber, because its members are **democratically elected**; the PM and almost all the cabinet are MPs | |
| What MPs do | Represent **everyone** in their constituency; help to **create new laws**; **scrutinise** and comment on what the government does; **debate** important national issues | Four duties |
| The House of Lords | Members, called **peers**, are **not elected**{: .hy} and **do not represent a constituency**; normally **more independent** of the government than the Commons | |
| Peers until 1958 | All peers were **hereditary** (inherited title), **senior judges**, or **bishops** of the Church of England | |
| Life peers (since 1958) | The **PM** can nominate peers for **their own lifetime**{: .hy}; appointed by the **monarch** on the PM's advice; others are nominated by other party leaders or an independent **Appointments Commission** | Most Lords are now life peers |
| Hereditary peers (since 1999) | Lost the **automatic right** to attend; they now **elect a few** of their number | 1999 |
| What the Lords does | Suggests **amendments** or proposes new laws; **checks** laws passed by the Commons; holds the government to account | The Commons can **overrule** the Lords, though rarely does |
| The Speaker | Chairs debates in the Commons; its **chief officer**; **neutral**, though an MP with a constituency; chosen by MPs in a **secret ballot**{: .hy}; keeps order and guarantees the opposition time | |
| General Election | Held **at least every five years**{: .hy} | |
| By-election | A fresh election in one constituency when an MP **dies or resigns**{: .hy} | |
| First past the post | In each constituency, the candidate with the **most votes**{: .hy} is elected; the party winning most constituencies usually forms the government | No majority: a **coalition** |
| Contacting your MP | By letter, phone or email; at local **"surgeries"**{: .hy}, often advertised in local newspapers | Surgeries = meet in person. Today: find your MP and their contact details with Parliament's [Find your MP](https://members.parliament.uk/FindYourMP) service (checked online, November 2026) |
| Hansard | Proceedings in Parliament are **broadcast on television** and published in official reports called **Hansard** | |
| The media | The UK has a **free press**{: .hy}: newspapers are free from government control; radio and TV coverage of the parties must be **balanced** | |

The 2013 text also described elections to the European Parliament (MEPs, every five years). TSO says the EU sections were removed from later prints; the UK left the EU on 31 January 2020 ([TSO](https://tsoshop.co.uk/article/636421/Life-in-the-United-Kingdom-A-Guide-for-New-Residents-3rd-Edition)).
{: .note}

**Lords dates**: until **1958** all peers were hereditary, judges or bishops; from **1958**, life peers; from **1999**, hereditary peers lost their automatic seat.
{: .hook}

## 4. The government
{: .era-4}

The party with the most MPs forms the government, led by the Prime Minister.

| Topic | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Prime Minister | Leader of the party in power; appoints the cabinet and controls many public appointments | Can be changed by the governing party's MPs; usually resigns after losing a General Election |
| PM's homes | Official home: **10 Downing Street**{: .hy}, central London; country house: **Chequers** | |
| The cabinet | About **20 senior MPs** in charge of departments; usually meets **weekly**; many decisions are debated or approved by Parliament | |
| Ministers of State and Parliamentary Under-Secretaries of State | Other ministers in each department, in charge of particular areas | |
| The opposition | The **second-largest party** in the Commons; its leader usually becomes PM if it wins the next election | |
| Prime Minister's Questions | Every **week**{: .hy} while Parliament is sitting | The opposition leader's big chance |
| Shadow cabinet | Senior opposition MPs appointed as **"shadow ministers"** to challenge the government | |
| Standing as an MP | Anyone **18 or over** can stand, but is unlikely to win without a major party | |
| Main parties | **Conservative**, **Labour**, **Liberal Democrats**{: .hy}, and parties for Scottish, Welsh or Northern Irish interests | |
| Independents | MPs who do not represent any main party, usually standing on a local issue | |
| Pressure and lobby groups | Try to **influence government policy**{: .hy}: e.g. the **CBI** (British business), **Greenpeace** (environment), **Liberty** (human rights) | |
| The civil service | Supports the government and delivers public services; accountable to ministers; chosen on merit; **politically neutral**{: .hy}, not political appointees | Core values: **integrity, honesty, objectivity, impartiality** |
| Local government | Towns, cities and rural areas are run by elected councils (**local authorities**{: .hy}); some areas have district and county councils; funded by **central government** and **local taxes** | Local elections for most are in **May** every year |
| Mayors | Many councils appoint a **ceremonial** mayor; some towns **elect** a mayor to lead | London: **33** local authorities, the Greater London Authority and the Mayor of London |

| Role | Responsible for | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chancellor of the Exchequer | **The economy**{: .hy} | A cabinet member |
| Home Secretary | **Crime, policing and immigration**{: .hy} | Home = inside the UK |
| Foreign Secretary | Managing **relationships with foreign countries** | |
| Secretaries of State | Subjects such as **education, health and defence** | |

| Body | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Devolution | Since **1997**, some powers devolved to give Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland **more control over matters that directly affect them**{: .hy}; a Welsh Assembly and Scottish Parliament since **1999** | Each has its own civil service |
| Kept by the UK government | **Defence, foreign affairs, immigration, taxation, social security** | Many other services, e.g. **education**, are devolved |
| The Senedd (Welsh Parliament) | In **Cardiff**{: .hy}; **60** Members of the Senedd (**MSs**; older printings: Assembly members, AMs); elections every **four** years by proportional representation; members speak Welsh or English; laws in **20** areas, e.g. education and training, health and social services, economic development, housing | Since **2011** it can pass laws on these without the UK Parliament's agreement |
| Scottish Parliament | Formed in **1999**; sits in **Edinburgh**{: .hy} (Holyrood); **129** MSPs, elected by proportional representation; can pass laws on **all matters not reserved** to the UK Parliament, e.g. civil and criminal law, health, education, planning, additional tax-raising powers | |
| Northern Ireland Parliament | Established in **1922** when Ireland was divided; **abolished in 1972**, shortly after the Troubles broke out in 1969 | |
| Northern Ireland Assembly | Established soon after the **Belfast (Good Friday) Agreement** of **1998**; **power-sharing**; **108** MLAs, by proportional representation; decides e.g. education, **agriculture**, the environment, health, **social services**{: .hy} | The UK government can suspend devolved assemblies; NI's has run successfully since **2007** |
| Buildings | Welsh Assembly building opened **March 2006**; Scottish Parliament building opened **October 2004**; NI Assembly building: **Stormont** | From the handbook's photo captions |

**Who sits where**: Wales in **Cardiff** (60 MSs), Scotland in **Edinburgh** (129 MSPs), Northern Ireland at **Stormont** in Belfast (108 MLAs). The UK government keeps defence, foreign affairs, immigration, taxation and social security.
{: .hook}

## 5. Voting and standing for office
{: .era-5}

The UK has had a fully democratic voting system since **1928**, and the present voting age of **18** was set in **1969**.

| Topic | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who can vote | UK-born and naturalised adult citizens; and **citizens of the Commonwealth and Ireland**{: .hy} resident in the UK, in all public elections | |
| The electoral register | You must be **on the register**{: .hy} to vote; register through your **local council electoral registration office** | Today: register **online** at [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/register-to-vote) in about 5 minutes; you normally register only once (checked online, November 2026) |
| Updating the register | Every year in **September or October**; a form goes to **every household** | |
| Northern Ireland | **"Individual registration"**{: .hy}: everyone entitled to vote completes their own form | |
| Seeing the register | Each local authority must make it available to anyone (supervised); also at some libraries | |
| Where to vote | **Polling stations** (**polling places** in Scotland); you get a **poll card**; open **7.00 am to 10.00 pm** | |
| At the polling station | Give your name and address (in Northern Ireland, also **photo ID**); mark your ballot paper privately in a polling booth; no one can make you vote for a particular candidate | |
| Postal ballot | Register for one if it's hard to get to a polling station | |
| Today (not in the handbook) | **Photo ID** has been needed to vote in person in **Great Britain** too since **May 2023** ([LSE](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/politicsandpolicy/no-id-no-vote-voter-id-comes-to-great-britain)): at UK parliamentary elections, local elections in England and some others ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/how-to-vote/photo-id-youll-need), checked online, November 2026) | |
| Standing for office | Most citizens of the **UK, Ireland or the Commonwealth** aged **18 or over**{: .hy} | |
| Who cannot stand | Members of the **armed forces**, **civil servants**, people found guilty of certain criminal offences | Members of the Lords cannot stand for the Commons |
| Visiting Parliament | Watch debates from the **public galleries** of both Houses; **entrance is free**{: .hy}; get tickets from your MP or **queue** on the day | The Commons queue can be 1 to 2 hours; the Lords is easier |
| Visiting the devolved bodies | **Stormont** (via the Education Service or an MLA); the Scottish Parliament at **Holyrood** (tickets and tours through visitor services, [parliament.scot](https://www.parliament.scot/visit)); the **Senedd** building in **Cardiff Bay** | |

The 2013 text also said adult citizens of other EU states living in the UK could vote in all elections except General Elections. That is EU material, which TSO says was removed from later prints.
{: .note}

**Commonwealth and Irish citizens** living in the UK can vote in all public elections, and most can stand for office at 18.
{: .hook}

## 6. The UK and international institutions
{: .era-6}

The UK is a member of the Commonwealth, the Council of Europe, the United Nations and NATO.

| Body | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Commonwealth | An association of countries working towards shared goals in **democracy and development**; most were once part of the **British Empire** | Membership is **voluntary**; it has **no power** over members but can **suspend** them |
| Its head and size | The monarch is its **ceremonial head**{: .hy}; **54** member states (2013) | Australia and Canada are members |
| Its values | **Democracy, good government and the rule of law**{: .hy} | |
| The Council of Europe | **Separate from the EU**; **46** members in the current printing (47 in older printings; Russia stopped being a member in 2022, [EEAS](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/council-europe/committee-ministers-decides-russia-ceases-be-member-council-europe_en)), including the UK; protects and promotes **human rights**; **cannot make laws**, but draws up conventions and charters | Best known: the **European Convention on Human Rights** |
| The United Nations | More than **190** countries; set up **after the Second World War**{: .hy} to prevent war and promote peace and security | |
| The UN Security Council | **15** members; the UK is one of **five permanent** members | |
| NATO | European and North American countries that agree to **help each other if attacked**{: .hy}, and keep peace between members | |

The handbook's Commonwealth list names all 54 members in 2013, including Antigua and Barbuda, Australia, The Bahamas, Bangladesh, Barbados, Belize, Botswana, Brunei Darussalam, Cameroon, Canada, Cyprus, Dominica, Fiji (then suspended), The Gambia, Ghana, Grenada, Guyana, India, Jamaica, Kenya, Kiribati, Lesotho, Malawi, Malaysia, Maldives, Malta, Mauritius, Mozambique, Namibia, Nauru, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, Papua New Guinea, Rwanda, Samoa, Seychelles, Sierra Leone, Singapore, Solomon Islands, South Africa, Sri Lanka, St Kitts and Nevis, St Lucia, St Vincent and the Grenadines, Swaziland, Tanzania, Tonga, Trinidad and Tobago, Tuvalu, Uganda, the UK, Vanuatu and Zambia. The 2019 printing adds that the Maldives left the Commonwealth in October 2016.
{: .note}

The 2013 text also had a section on the European Union (the Treaty of Rome in 1957, the UK joining in 1973). TSO says the EU sections were removed from later prints, and the UK left the EU on 31 January 2020. The 1973 joining date is still in Chapter 3.
{: .note}

**Council of Europe ≠ EU.** The Council of Europe looks after human rights and makes no laws; its best-known product is the **European Convention on Human Rights**.
{: .hook}

## 7. Respecting the law
{: .era-7}

Everyone in the UK receives **equal treatment under the law**, and knowing and obeying it is one of every resident's most important responsibilities.

| Topic | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criminal law | **Crimes**, usually investigated by the **police** or another authority such as a council, and **punished by the courts**{: .hy} | |
| Civil law | Settles **disputes**{: .hy} between individuals or groups | |
| Criminal: weapons | Carrying a weapon of **any kind**, even for self-defence | |
| Criminal: drugs | Selling or buying drugs such as heroin, cocaine, ecstasy and cannabis | |
| Criminal: racial crime | Causing harassment, alarm or distress because of someone's **religion or ethnic origin** | |
| Criminal: tobacco | Selling tobacco to anyone **under 18** | |
| Criminal: smoking | Smoking in nearly every **enclosed public place** | |
| Criminal: alcohol | Selling alcohol to, or buying it for, anyone **under 18** | Exception: **16+** can drink with a meal in a hotel or restaurant |
| Criminal: drinking in public | Not allowed in **alcohol-free zones**; police can confiscate alcohol | |
| Civil: examples | **Housing** (landlords and tenants), **consumer rights** (faulty goods), **employment** (wages, unfair dismissal, discrimination), **debt** | Debt = civil |
| The police's job | **Protect life and property**; **prevent disturbances** (keep the peace); **prevent and detect crime** | Three duties |
| Police forces | Headed by **Chief Constables**; **independent of the government** | |
| Police and Crime Commissioners | First elected in **November 2012**{: .hy} in **England and Wales**; set local priorities and budget; appoint the Chief Constable | |
| PCSOs | **Police community support officers** patrol, work with the public and support officers | |
| Police behaviour | Police must **obey the law**; must not misuse authority, make false statements, be rude or abusive, or commit racial discrimination | |
| Complaints | To a police station or the Chief Constable; or an independent body: the **Independent Office for Police Conduct** (England and Wales); in Scotland, if unhappy with how a complaint was handled, the **Police Investigations and Review Commissioner**; in Northern Ireland, the **Police Ombudsman** | Older printings: the Independent Police Complaints Commission |
| Terrorism | The most serious threat: **Al Qa'ida**, its affiliates and like-minded groups; also Northern Ireland-related terrorism | Tell your local police if someone tries to recruit you |
| Extremism | Vocal or active opposition to **fundamental British values**; includes religious and far-right extremism | |

## 8. The courts

## 11. Commonly confused
{: .era-none}

The government and law pairs that trip people up.

| Pair | Tell them apart |
| --- | --- |
| **House of Commons** vs **House of Lords** | Commons: elected MPs, the more important chamber. Lords: unelected peers, more independent, can be overruled |
| **General Election** vs **by-election** | Whole country, at least every five years vs one constituency, when an MP dies or resigns |
| **Speaker** vs **Prime Minister** | Speaker: neutral MP chosen by MPs in a secret ballot to chair debates. PM: leader of the party in power |
| **Chancellor** vs **Home Secretary** vs **Foreign Secretary** | Economy vs crime, policing and immigration vs relationships with foreign countries |
| **Opposition** vs **shadow cabinet** | The second-largest party vs the senior opposition MPs who shadow ministers |
| **Pressure groups** vs **political parties** | Influence policy (CBI, Greenpeace, Liberty) vs stand for election |
| **Civil servants** vs **ministers** | Politically neutral and chosen on merit vs politicians in charge of departments |
| **Life peers** vs **hereditary peers** | Appointed for their lifetime (since 1958) vs inherited titles, no automatic right to sit since 1999 |
| **Scottish Parliament** vs **Senedd** vs **NI Assembly** | Edinburgh, 129 MSPs vs Cardiff, 60 MSs vs Stormont, 108 MLAs |
| **Council of Europe** vs **the EU** | Human rights, makes no laws vs (2013 text, now removed) EU law binding on members |
| **UN Security Council** | 15 members; the UK is one of 5 permanent members |
| **Criminal law** vs **civil law** | Crimes punished by the courts vs disputes between people or groups (housing, consumer, employment, debt) |
| **Magistrates' Court** vs **Crown Court** | Minor crimes, unpaid magistrates vs serious crimes, judge and jury |
| **County Court** vs **High Court** | Civil disputes vs serious civil cases (Court of Session in Scotland) |
| **Jury of 12** vs **jury of 15** | England, Wales and NI vs Scotland |
| **Youth Court** vs **Children's Hearings System** | Ages 10 to 17 in England, Wales and NI vs Scotland's system |
| **Income tax** vs **National Insurance** | Pays for roads, education, police, armed forces vs pays for benefits, the state pension and the NHS |
| **PAYE** vs **self-assessment** | Employer deducts tax vs you complete a tax return |
| **17** vs **16** for vehicles | Car or motor cycle vs moped |
| **Oath** vs **affirmation** of allegiance | Sworn "by Almighty God" vs solemnly declared without a religious reference |

If a law question mentions a dispute between people (housing, faulty goods, wages, debt), it's **civil**. If it mentions police or punishment, it's **criminal**.
{: .hook}
