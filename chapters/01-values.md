# Chapter 1: Values and principles cheat sheet

<p class="lead">The three lists of five (principles, responsibilities, freedoms), the citizenship pledge, and what the handbook says about settling in the UK and taking the test.</p>

## How to use this

This sheet follows Chapter 1 of the official handbook, *Life in the United Kingdom: A guide for new residents* (3rd edition), still the only testable source.

- Chapter 1 has almost no dates, so most tables here are numbered lists with an exam hook for each item.
- **Bold** = a key word or number. Gold rows marked **★ Often asked** = what comes up most in practice tests. This is based on unofficial study guides, as the Home Office does not publish question data.
- The handbook was written in 2013. Where the rules have changed since, the row says **Today (not in the handbook)**. Newer printings of the handbook may already include some of these changes, so if your copy differs from this sheet, follow your copy.

Principles are **ideas** (Democracy, Liberty...). Responsibilities start with a **verb** for something you do (Respect, Treat, Look after). Freedoms start with **Freedom** or **A right**.
{: .hook}

## 1. Where the values come from
{: .era-1}

British society is founded on fundamental values that everyone living in the UK should respect and support.

| Topic | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Based on | **History and traditions** | The values come from the past |
| Protected by | **Law, customs and expectations** | Three protections: law, customs, expectations |
| Reflected in | The **responsibilities, rights and privileges** of being a British citizen or permanent resident | What you must do and what you get both come from the values |
| No place for | **Extremism**{: .hy} or **intolerance** | British society has no place for either |
| Citizenship ceremony | New citizens **pledge** to uphold these values | The pledge is below |

The citizenship pledge, word for word:

> I will give my loyalty to the United Kingdom and respect its rights and freedoms. I will uphold its democratic values. I will observe its laws faithfully and fulfil my duties and obligations as a British citizen.

The pledge has five promises in order: **loyalty**, respect **rights and freedoms**, uphold **democratic values**, observe **laws**, fulfil **duties**.
{: .hook}

## 2. The five fundamental principles
{: .era-2}

These are the fundamental principles of British life, and the list most worth knowing by heart.

| # | Principle | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Democracy**{: .hy} | **D**on't. Not the same as *a right to join in the election of a government*, which is a freedom |
| 2 | **The rule of law**{: .hy} | **R**un. Not the same as *respect and obey the law*, which is a responsibility |
| 3 | **Individual liberty**{: .hy} | **I**n. Not the same as *freedom of speech*, which is a freedom |
| 4 | **Tolerance of those with different faiths and beliefs**{: .hy} | **T**he. Not the same as *freedom of belief and religion*, which is a freedom |
| 5 | **Participation in community life**{: .hy} | **P**ark. Not the same as *look after the area in which you live*, which is a responsibility |

**D**on't **R**un **I**n **T**he **P**ark: Democracy, Rule of law, Individual liberty, Tolerance, Participation.
{: .hook}

## 3. Responsibilities: what you should do
{: .era-3}

If you wish to be a permanent resident or citizen of the UK, the handbook says you should do these five things.

| # | Responsibility | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Respect and obey **the law** | Not *the rule of law*, which is a principle |
| 2 | Respect **the rights of others**, including their right to their own opinions | Includes their right to *their own opinions* |
| 3 | Treat others with **fairness** | Not *freedom from unfair discrimination*, which is a freedom |
| 4 | Look after **yourself and your family** | Look after #1: you, then your family |
| 5 | Look after **the area in which you live** and **the environment** | Not *participation in community life*, which is a principle |

Two *respects*, one *treat*, two *look afters*: you respect the law and others, treat others fairly, and look after your family and your area.
{: .hook}

## 4. Freedoms: what the UK offers in return
{: .era-5}

In return for those responsibilities, the UK offers five freedoms and rights.

| # | Freedom | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Freedom of **belief and religion** | Not *tolerance of those with different faiths and beliefs*, which is a principle |
| 2 | Freedom of **speech** | Not *individual liberty*, which is a principle |
| 3 | Freedom from **unfair discrimination** | *From*, not *of*. Not *treat others with fairness*, which is a responsibility |
| 4 | A right to a **fair trial** | A right, not a freedom: the first of the two rights |
| 5 | A right to join in the **election of a government** | Voting. Not *democracy*, which is a principle |

Three *freedoms* (belief and religion, speech, from unfair discrimination) and two *rights* (a fair trial, joining in the election of a government).
{: .hook}

## 5. Becoming a permanent resident
{: .era-6}

The handbook sets out what you need to settle in the UK, and how the rules changed in October 2013.

| When | Event | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January 2013 | To become a permanent resident or citizen you need to **speak and read English** and have a **good understanding of life in the UK** | The two requirements |
| January 2013 | **Route 1:** pass the **Life in the UK test**. Its questions need English at **ESOL Entry Level 3**, so no separate English test | One test covered both requirements |
| January 2013 | **Route 2:** if your English is below Entry Level 3, pass an **ESOL course in English with Citizenship**, which ends with a test | For people still learning English |
| **October 2013**{: .hy} | For **settlement or permanent residence**: pass the Life in the UK test **and** show **speaking and listening** skills in English at **B1** (CEFR), equivalent to **ESOL Entry Level 3** | Both, not either |
| October 2013 | Citizenship requirements "may also change in the future" | The handbook fixes only the settlement rule |
| Today (not in the handbook) | The rules have changed since 2013 | Check GOV.UK for the current requirements before you apply |

Before October 2013 the test alone could cover both requirements. From October 2013, settlement needs the test **plus** separate English evidence at B1.
{: .note}

**B1 = Entry Level 3**: the same level of English on two different scales. The evidence is about **speaking and listening**, while the requirement is to **speak and read** English.
{: .hook}

## 6. Taking the Life in the UK test
{: .era-none}

What the handbook says about the test itself.

| Topic | Handbook says | Exam hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Questions | **24**{: .hy} questions, different for each person at that test session | 24, like the hours in a day |
| Based on | **All parts**{: .hy} of the handbook, including the introduction | Every chapter can come up |
| Language | Usually **English**{: .hy}; special arrangements can be made for **Welsh** or **Scottish Gaelic** | English, Welsh or Scottish Gaelic only |
| Where | Only at a **registered and approved** Life in the UK test centre; **about 60** around the UK | 60 centres, 24 questions. Today: GOV.UK says over 30 centres |
| Booking | **Online only**, at www.lifeintheuktest.gov.uk (the handbook's address) | Today: book through GOV.UK, the only official booking service |
| What to bring | **Identification** and **proof of address**; without them you cannot take the test | Today: GOV.UK says bring the same original ID you booked with; your photo is taken on the day |
| "Check that you understand" boxes | A guide only: knowing just these **will not be enough to pass** | Read the whole handbook |
| Glossary | Key words and phrases, at the back of the handbook | Use it for unfamiliar terms |

Not in the 2013 handbook text. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test) says that today you have **45 minutes** for the 24 questions, you need **75%** to pass (18 of 24), it costs **£50**, you must book at least **3 days** ahead, and there are **over 30** test centres. You don't need to take the test if you are under 18 or 65 or over.
{: .note}

**24** questions, about **60** centres, bring **ID and proof of address**, in English, Welsh or Scottish Gaelic.
{: .hook}

## 7. Commonly confused
{: .era-none}

Most wrong answers in this chapter come from putting an item on the wrong list.

| Pair | Tell them apart |
| --- | --- |
| **The rule of law** vs **respect and obey the law** | Principle (an idea) vs responsibility (something you do) |
| **Democracy** vs **a right to join in the election of a government** | Principle vs freedom the UK offers |
| **Tolerance of those with different faiths and beliefs** vs **freedom of belief and religion** | Principle vs freedom |
| **Individual liberty** vs **freedom of speech** | Principle vs freedom |
| **Participation in community life** vs **look after the area in which you live** | Principle vs responsibility |
| **Treat others with fairness** vs **freedom from unfair discrimination** | Responsibility (you do it) vs freedom (the UK offers it) |
| **Freedom *of* speech** vs **freedom *from* unfair discrimination** | *Of* = something you can do; *from* = something you are protected against |
| **B1** vs **ESOL Entry Level 3** | The same level on two scales (CEFR and ESOL) |
| **Speak and read** English vs **speaking and listening** at B1 | The requirement vs the evidence needed from October 2013 |
| **Settlement** vs **citizenship** (October 2013) | The handbook applies the test-plus-B1 rule to settlement; citizenship rules "may also change" |
| **24 questions** vs **about 60 centres** | Questions in the test vs test centres in the UK |

If a statement starts with a verb (respect, treat, look after), it's a responsibility. If it starts with "freedom" or "a right", it's a freedom. Otherwise it's a principle.
{: .hook}
